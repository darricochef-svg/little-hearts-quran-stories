# Little Hearts — Animated Quran Story Production Standard

## Editorial promise
Every episode teaches a story that is actually grounded in the Qur'an. The Qur'an is the source; the cartoon is a child-friendly presentation, not an expansion of revelation. Keep the distinction clear between (1) Qur'anic facts, (2) simple explanations of those facts, and (3) fictional host framing.

## Audience and language
- Primary audience: ages 2–4, with simple Modern Standard Arabic narration.
- Short sentences, one idea per sentence, gentle repetition, concrete vocabulary.
- English subtitles are for parents/caregivers and must preserve meaning rather than embellish it.
- Do not simplify away theological meaning or turn metaphor into asserted fact.

## Source and accuracy workflow
For every story, create a source map before writing:
1. List the exact surah and ayah range.
2. Extract only the events explicitly established by those verses.
3. Mark explanatory context separately and identify its source.
4. Do not add dialogue, names, locations, motives, sequence details, or visual actions as Qur'anic facts unless supported by the source.
5. Have a qualified reviewer verify the Arabic, translation, context and lesson before publication.

## Visual and religious safeguards
- Never depict Allah or visually portray any prophet.
- Do not put invented dialogue in a prophet's mouth.
- Use neutral symbolic imagery where needed: landscape, sky, water, light, plants, objects, distant non-human silhouettes.
- Nour (cream rabbit) and Zuzu (golden bird) are fictional presenters only. They do not meet prophets or enter Qur'anic events.
- Clearly signal when the hosts are introducing or reflecting on a story, not witnessing it.
- Avoid frightening imagery, flashing, harsh cuts, overstimulation or misleading visual claims.

## Animation standard — no slideshow-only episodes
Every episode must include purposeful motion, not merely a still illustration with a zoom:
- Character body movement: walk, hop, turn, nod, wing/ear gestures, breathing and blinking.
- Dialogue performance: separate voice identities for Nour and Zuzu; mouth shapes change in sync with their own spoken lines.
- Narrator sections: hosts listen/react silently; no false lip-sync.
- Environment motion: water, clouds, leaves, bubbles, stars, gentle light and particles.
- Camera movement: restrained pans or tracking only when it supports the action.
- Maintain consistent character proportions, colors and accessories across all scenes.
- Keep motion calm and readable for preschool viewers.

## Audio and dialogue
- Narrator voice is distinct from the hosts.
- Give each host a consistent voice; do not use one undifferentiated TTS voice for all dialogue.
- Generate speech clips per speaker and line, then align mouth shapes to measured speech timings.
- Preserve natural pauses; never stretch a scene to an arbitrary fixed duration.
- Human Arabic speaker must listen to the complete cut for pronunciation, pacing, clarity and emotional tone.
- Quranic recitation must be checked independently and must not be casually paraphrased.

## Production pipeline / release gates
1. Qur'anic source map and reviewed script.
2. Approved character designs and storyboard.
3. Speaker-separated Arabic voice clips.
4. Character animation with timed mouth shapes and body/scene motion.
5. Scene-level edit with dialogue-timed subtitles.
6. Full playback review: Arabic, subtitles, animation, audio levels, child suitability.
7. Qualified religious review and final sign-off.
8. Thumbnail/metadata and parent-facing description.
9. Upload only after explicit owner approval.

## Episode 01 — Prophet Yunus
Qur'anic anchor: 21:87–88 and 37:139–148. Keep the exact dua from 21:87 accurate. The whale and rescue are central; do not invent a conversation inside the whale or depict Prophet Yunus. Do not claim extra details beyond the cited verses as Qur'anic facts. The existing Nour/Zuzu shore introduction is fictional host framing and must remain clearly outside the sacred narrative.
